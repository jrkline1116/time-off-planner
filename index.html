    What's available on a specific day?

    How hours post. Each paycheck adds that pay period's PTO and PSL. Time off is charged on the paycheck for the pay period it falls in, and that period's new hours can cover it. Balances can't go negative. Hours that don't fit show as short (unpaid).

    PTO rate by year. For salaried staff, your annual PTO = your rate × pay periods in that year. In a year with a different number of paychecks, the rate per check changes so the annual total stays the same. For hourly staff, the rate per check stays the same.

    PTO carryover. After the last paycheck of the year, anything over one year's PTO is forfeited.

    PSL. You earn 1 hour per 30 worked, up to 80 a year. You can use 40 a year, and up to 40 roll over to the next year.

    Floating holiday. 8 hours are issued on each weekend holiday and expire 12 months later; future ones are added for you. The hours you enter now aren't given an expiration date, so check yours.

    All time off. PTO + the PSL you're still allowed to use that year (40-hour limit) + floating hours.

    Not modeled yet. FMLA or other leave (no PTO earned, no holiday pay), the 90-day orientation period, and 5- and 10-year accrual increases (enter the new rate when it changes).
